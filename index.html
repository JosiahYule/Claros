About the Editor

Throughout my academic career I was told that I was a good writer. Academic writing is very often not the clearest or most pleasing to read, but I felt it important to affect a level of clarity and simplicity of style that would best convey the ideas I had in my head. Good writing does not announce itself as good writing, or reach for sophisticated diction without need. When the writing is good it gets out of its own way—complex ideas are rendered easy to digest—the writer and reader see eye-to-eye.

In 2017 I began editing academic documents for my peers. This included master’s theses and doctoral dissertations across the fields of politics, anthropology, history, sports medicine, religious studies, civil engineering, and philosophy, among others.

“Great is the stream of the Assyrian river, but much filth of earth and much refuse it carries on its waters. And not of every water do the Melissae carry to Deo, but of the trickling stream that springs from a holy fountain, pure and undefiled, the very crown of waters.”

Callimachus, Hymn to Apollo

In this passage, Callimachus uses water as a symbol for writing and poetry. The ‘Assyrian River’ is the Tigris in Mesopotamia, but it is here being used as a metaphor for Homer’s epics: a large flowing body of text that (in Callimachus’ mind) contained lots of bad writing. Instead, Callimachus boasts of his own ability to write beautiful, concise, and curated poetry—a holy fountain, ‘pure and undefiled’.
